Sim-to-Real Deployment for Bipedal Locomotion

01

Phase 1

Isaac Lab Simulation & Policy Design

Environment Architecture

Engineered scalable end-to-end RL training pipelines leveraging the full manager-based architecture of NVIDIA Isaac Lab — comprehensively configuring observation/action managers, multi-term reward shaping, curriculum learning, and extensive domain randomization.

Taming Policy-Induced Vibration

The Challenge

Initial end-to-end policies generated overly dramatic actions, triggering violent vibrations and instability upon real-world deployment.

The Mitigation

Implemented strict action-rate penalties and multi-component reward shaping — balancing gait phase synchronization, action smoothness, and foot clearance height.

Asymmetric Actor-Critic Design

  • ▸Injected privileged information (such as true linear velocity) into the Critic during training to accelerate convergence.
  • ▸Restricted the Actor to angular velocity and local observations, bypassing the reality that precise linear velocity is difficult to estimate accurately on real hardware without high-end localization.

02

Phase 2

The Hardware Wall & The Failure of Pure RL

Actuator & Drivetrain Constraints

Pure end-to-end RL policies that succeed on robots with simpler, direct actuation don't necessarily transfer with basic delay randomization alone. Our platform — a proprietary humanoid with linear-actuator-driven parallel-linkage legs — introduced additional actuator latency, bandwidth limits, and unmodeled dynamics that a generic sim-to-real recipe could not close.

Failure Hypothesis

Pure end-to-end RL struggled to fully capture the drivetrain non-linearities and complex transmission dynamics of the parallel-linkage leg joints simply by tuning delay randomization in simulation — closing that gap required rigorous system identification of the true actuator dynamics.

Sim-to-Real Actuator Latency Parallel-Linkage Dynamics Transmission Non-Linearity Actuator Dynamics
03

Phase 3

The Hybrid Control Breakthrough & Solution

To conquer what pure RL couldn't, we engineered a rigorous hybrid pipeline:

1 · Low-Level Analytical Solvers & Safety

  • ▸Developed a CasADi-based virtual work solver (generated C++) for the parallel-linkage leg joints, running inside a 1 kHz real-time control loop.
  • ▸Implemented active mechanism determinant checks to reject near-singular configurations before torque dispatch.

2 · Impedance Control & Bandwidth Tuning

  • ▸Architected an impedance control pipeline for the parallel-linkage leg joints, applying joint-level force control to compensate for actuator non-linearities.
  • ▸Validated tuning via chirp-sweep frequency response to identify optimal proportional (Kp) and derivative (Kd) gains.

3 · Robustness Hardening

  • ▸Simulated delayed PD actuators via domain randomization over actuator dynamics and latency, alongside link and base center-of-mass variations, aligned with the 1 kHz real-time control loop.

4 · The Ultimate Solution — Residual Learning via LIPM

  • ▸System Identification: performed joint-level actuator characterization via chirp-sweep testing to measure closed-loop response and bandwidth limits, directly informing training and control design.
  • ▸The Hybrid Strategy: utilized an analytical Linear Inverted Pendulum Model (LIPM) as the primary trajectory generator, commanding a smooth gait trajectory within the actuator's characterized bandwidth.
  • ▸The Result: deployed a model-free residual policy to handle unmodeled disturbances, achieving 30 minutes of continuous walking without operator intervention or controller resets.

Junction Modeling

Geometric Polygon Junction Modeling

At complex intersections, merging lanes cross and overlap rather than remaining strictly adjacent — causing traditional lane-following algorithms to detect insufficient driving space and fail. Using behavior planning, redesigned intersection navigation by modeling junctions as geometric polygons that break free from strict HD-map lane boundaries within the intersection area, enabling natural, flexible driving behavior similar to human navigation through open junction spaces.
