01

Phase 1

Isaac Lab Simulation & Policy Design

Environment Architecture

Engineered scalable end-to-end RL training pipelines leveraging the full manager-based architecture of NVIDIA Isaac Lab — comprehensively configuring observation/action managers, multi-term reward shaping, curriculum learning, and extensive domain randomization.

Taming Policy-Induced Vibration

The Challenge

Initial end-to-end policies generated overly dramatic actions, triggering violent vibrations and instability upon real-world deployment.

The Mitigation

Implemented strict action-rate penalties and multi-component reward shaping — balancing gait phase synchronization, action smoothness, and foot clearance height.

Asymmetric Actor-Critic Design

  • ▸Injected privileged information (such as true linear velocity) into the Critic during training to accelerate convergence.
  • ▸Restricted the Actor to angular velocity and local observations, bypassing the reality that precise linear velocity is difficult to estimate accurately on real hardware without high-end localization.

02

Phase 2

The Hardware Wall & The Failure of Pure RL

The Hardware Bottleneck — QDD vs. High-Reduction Actuator

While robots utilizing clean QDD (Quasi-Direct Drive) motors can often achieve successful sim-to-real transfer with pure end-to-end RL and basic delay randomization, our platform — driven by a high-reduction actuator architecture combining 28:1 planetary gear rotary motors and linear motors with planetary gears — presented a massive wall.

Failure Hypothesis

Pure end-to-end RL failed on this hardware because it is virtually impossible to fully capture such intricate drivetrain non-linearities, high backlash, and complex transmission dynamics simply by tuning delay randomization in simulation — closing that gap required rigorous system identification of the true actuator dynamics.

Sim-to-Real QDD vs. High-Reduction Actuator Planetary Gearbox Transmission Non-Linearity High Backlash Actuator Dynamics
03

Phase 3

The Hybrid Control Breakthrough & Solution

To conquer what pure RL couldn't, we engineered a rigorous hybrid pipeline:

1 · Low-Level Analytical Solvers & Safety

  • ▸Developed a CasADi-based virtual work solver (generated C++) running in under 10 µs inside a 1 kHz real-time control loop, handling complex kinematics for 4-bar linkages and parallel mechanisms per joint.
  • ▸Implemented active mechanism determinant checks to reject near-singular configurations before any torque dispatch, preventing hardware self-destruction.

2 · Impedance Control & Bandwidth Tuning

  • ▸Architected an impedance control pipeline for non-linear parallel mechanisms, applying joint-level force control to compensate for linear-actuator non-linearities.
  • ▸Validated tuning via chirp-sweep frequency response to identify optimal proportional (Kp) and derivative (Kd) gains.

3 · Robustness Hardening

  • ▸Simulated delayed PD actuators by randomizing timing delays (1–6 time steps) alongside link and base CoM variations, aligning with the 0.001 s (dt) interval of the 1000 Hz real-time loop.

4 · The Ultimate Solution — Residual Learning via LIPM

  • ▸System Identification: performed joint-level ID, revealing a minimum un-delayed response time around 1 Hz and an actuator bandwidth near 5 Hz.
  • ▸The Hybrid Strategy: utilized an analytical Linear Inverted Pendulum Model (LIPM) as the primary trajectory generator, commanding a smooth 0.8 Hz trajectory with a 5 cm foot height — well within actuator bandwidth.
  • ▸The Result: deployed a model-free residual policy to handle unmodeled disturbances, successfully achieving prolonged, continuous stable bipedal walking.
